Chapter 4 brings Bayesian inference into one of the most important models in applied statistics: linear regression. Here, the goal is not merely to fit a straight line through the data, but to describe a data-generating mechanism that can explain the relationship between input variables and outcome variables, while quantifying the uncertainty of the parameters and the predictions.

In this chapter, you will start from writing Bayesian linear regression as a generative model, and then learn how to choose principled priors for the regression coefficients. Next, the chapter guides posterior inference with PyMC to move from theoretical formulas to practice, and closes with model checking and prediction to evaluate whether the model truly fits the data and the analysis goal.

The goal of the chapter is to help you form a complete workflow for Bayesian regression: state the model, set priors, infer the posterior, check the model, and use the model to forecast. This is a direct foundation for the following chapters, where models become richer in structure and closer to real-world data problems.

After the required lessons, the optional lesson Lesson 4.7: Modern MCMC — NUTS, HMC and Bayesian Regression in Practice connects the same generative model with NUTS in Stan/PyMC/NumPyro, Pathfinder, BlackJAX, and MCMC for Bayesian neural networks. This lesson does not rewrite Markov chain theory.
