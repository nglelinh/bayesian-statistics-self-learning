Chapter 6 introduces Bayesian generalized linear models, extending the idea of regression to types of data for which the Gaussian assumption no longer fits. In practice, the outcome is often not a continuous, normally distributed quantity: sometimes it is a binary label, sometimes the number of times an event occurs. In that case, we need to change both the observation distribution and the way predictors are related to the outcome.

In this chapter, you will learn logistic regression for binary outcomes and Poisson regression for count data. These two models show that the same Bayesian way of thinking can be carried out on very different data spaces, as long as we describe the data-generating process correctly. The last part of the chapter focuses on model evaluation for GLMs to help you check whether the model fits the characteristics of the outcome and the prediction goal.

The goal of the chapter is to help you choose a model that suits the form of the data instead of forcing the data into a familiar mold. After this chapter, you will have the foundation to handle classification and count-data problems in a way that is consistent with Bayesian inference.

After the required lessons, the optional lesson Lesson 6.4: Modern Applications of Bayesian GLMs follows logistic and Poisson models into credit, demand, and nowcasting, along with Bayesian media mix modeling (Meridian). This lesson does not rewrite the link function.
