Chapter 1 opens with an important question: why do we need an approach different from traditional statistical inference? From the context of the replication crisis in science and the limits of p-values, this chapter leads you to the need to see probability not only as long-run frequency, but also as the plausibility of a hypothesis before and after observing data.

In this chapter, you will move from a critique of how classical hypothesis testing is used to the foundations of thinking in Bayesian inference. The lessons in turn explain probability as plausibility, present Bayes' theorem as a rule for updating beliefs, and compare the Bayesian view with the frequentist one to see clearly which kinds of questions each school answers better.

The goal of the chapter is to help you change how you pose statistical questions. Instead of only asking whether the data "rejects" a hypothesis, you will begin to learn to ask how the probabilities of hypotheses change as more data arrives, and why interpreting uncertainty is central to Bayesian analysis.

After the required lessons, the optional lesson Lesson 1.5: Modern Applications of Bayes' Theorem in Computer Science follows the same updating rule into spam and scam filtering, payment fraud, and the quantification of uncertainty in language models. This lesson does not rewrite the theory of Chapter 1.
