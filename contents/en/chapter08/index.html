Chapter 8 answers a key question once a model has been built: how do you know whether the model is good, and which model should you use for your analysis goal? In Bayesian analysis, estimating the posterior is only part of the work. A good model must also be checked, compared, and placed in the context of decision-making under uncertainty.

In this chapter, you will start with posterior predictive checks to learn how to criticize a model using the very data it generates. The lessons on WAIC, LOO and model comparison strategies then help you assess out-of-sample predictive ability instead of looking only at the fit to the data at hand. The chapter ends with Bayesian decision analysis, where inference results are tied directly to action through loss, utility, and optimal choice under risk.

The goal of the chapter is to help you move past the habit of "fit the model and stop". After this chapter, you should be able to check a model in a principled way, compare competing models on the basis of predictive goals, and understand that statistical inference ultimately has to serve practical decisions.

After the required lessons, the optional lesson Lesson 8.5: Modern Applications of Model Checking and Bayesian Decision-Making connects PPC with simulation-based calibration, LOO with the uncertainty of the comparison itself, and decision theory with Bayesian optimization and bandits. It does not redefine ELPD or the Bayes action.
