Chapter 5 extends regression from one predictor to many predictors, while focusing on interpreting models in a context where the variables are related to one another. When working with real data, it is very rare for a variable to operate completely in isolation; understanding the effects of confounding, multicollinearity and interaction is therefore essential if you want meaningful conclusions from a model.

In this chapter, you will learn multiple regression as a tool for describing several sources of variation at once, then use DAGs to think more clearly about causal structure and confounding paths. The following lessons help you recognize the problem of strongly correlated predictors, understand why regression coefficients can become unstable, and analyze interaction effects to see that the effect of one variable can depend on the level of another.

The goal of the chapter is to train you to read a regression model carefully and in context. After this chapter, you will not only know how to add variables to a model, but also understand when a coefficient can be interpreted, when to be cautious with causal language, and why the structure of the data matters as much as the estimation method itself.

After the required lessons, the optional lesson Lesson 5.5: Modern Applications of Multiple Regression and Causal Thinking brings DAGs, confounding and interactions into product experiments, structure learning (DAGMA), and counterfactual estimation over time. This lesson does not redraw the back-door theory.
